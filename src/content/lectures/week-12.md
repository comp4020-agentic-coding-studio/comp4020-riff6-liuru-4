Week 11 argued that "authentic" is a working distinction, not a fact waiting
to be dug up. That is a reason to hold a verdict carefully, not a reason to
stop giving one: a museum still has to decide what a wall label says, a
collector still has to decide what to pay, and a scholar still has to decide
what claim to put their name behind in print. The whole semester has been
building the evidence a verdict like that should rest on; this lecture is
about the last piece, which is presentation --- saying plainly what you
believe, how confident you are, and exactly what would change your mind.

The final project asks you to do that once, in full, on a painting you
choose from the semester's shortlist or bring from your own reading, and
then defend it live against questions from the room. The defense is not a
formality tacked onto the writing: it's where an argument that only looked
solid on paper usually gets found out, which is the same thing a colophon
or a seal is for.

## Outline

- what the final project's dossier-plus-defense format is actually testing
- how to state a verdict with calibrated confidence, and name what would

- the semester's toolkit, reviewed once as a checklist rather than twelve

- how the defenses run, and what happens after they do
