## The brief

> Choose one disputed painting --- from the semester's shortlist or your own
> reading --- and produce a full dossier on it, then defend your verdict
> live against the room.

This is the semester's actual capstone: not a new format, but the dossier's
discipline done once more, on a painting you've chosen rather than been
assigned, under the added pressure of a verdict you have to speak and
defend rather than only write. Everything from weeks 1--11 --- materials,
brushwork, seals, colophons, the market, computational methods, the forger's
own perspective, and week 11's reframing of what "authentic" is even asking
for --- is fair material for both the dossier and the questions you'll be
asked.

A good defense names its own weakest point before someone else does. If
your dossier's verdict rests heavily on one seal reading, expect the room
to go straight for it, and expect to have an answer ready that isn't "the
seal is probably fine."

## What you submit

The written dossier (as a single document, no fixed length beyond what the
argument needs) submitted by the deadline above, plus the live defense in
the week-12 seminar --- both are required, and neither substitutes for the
other. Declare any AI or outside research assistance per the
[policies page](/policies/).
