## The brief

> Defend the verdict from your authentication dossier, live, for ten
> minutes, in the week-12 seminar.

There is nothing new to write. This assessment takes the dossier you
submitted in week 8 and asks whether you actually hold the position it
reached, or whether the document was assembled rather than argued. Those
look identical on paper and completely different under a question.

Open by stating the verdict and its confidence level in one sentence, then
go straight to the weakest part of your own case. Everything from weeks
1--11 is fair material for the questions --- materials, brushwork, seals,
colophons, the market, what a model can and cannot see, the forger's own
perspective, and week 11's reframing of what "authentic" is even asking
for.

## Naming your own weakest point

A good defence gets there before the room does. If your verdict rests
heavily on one seal reading, expect the questions to go straight for it,
and expect an answer better than "the seal is probably fine." The
strongest defences tend to sound like: *this rests on the seal; here is
why I weight it above the colophon evidence that cuts the other way; and
here is the single finding that would make me withdraw it.*

Changing your mind under questioning is not a failure. Withdrawing a
verdict because one question was asked firmly is. The distinction the
marking is looking for is whether your confidence moves in proportion to
what the question actually established.

## What happens in the room

Ten minutes each, in the week-12 seminar: roughly three to present the
verdict and its weakest point, and the rest for questions from the room
and the convenor. Your dossier is the text under discussion, so bring it
and expect the questions to cite it.

The seminar closes by re-sorting the same six reproductions from week 1.
Declare any AI or outside research assistance used in preparing the
defence per the [policies page](/policies/).
